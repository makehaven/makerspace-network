Standards of Excellence

—

Self-assess. Set a goal. Benchmark with the network.

This tool helps any makerspace — regardless of size or program mix — determine which standards apply, assess current practice against them, build a prioritized improvement plan, and compare results across a network of peer spaces.

Describe your space. Answer the Profile questions. Core standards always apply; conditional modules (Youth, Tool Lending, Incubation…) switch on only when they match how you operate.
Score each applicable standard 0–3. 0 Absent · 1 Foundational · 2 Operational · 3 Sustained & review-ready. Record specific evidence — a document, URL, log, or verifiable observation.
Review the Dashboard. See your recognition level, domain scores, evidence coverage, and what stands between you and the next level.
Set a goal and work the Action Plan. Pick a target recognition level; the plan lists exactly which standards block it, with owners and due dates.
Share and benchmark. Export a network share file — profile, annual data, capabilities, and summary readiness only. A network coordinator loads everyone's share files in the Network Benchmark tab to see the collective picture.
Score honestly — the detail stays private. Your per-standard scores, evidence, and action plan never leave your space unless you choose to share the full private export. The network share file carries only summary readiness, and network views show readiness in aggregate — no space is ranked or individually scored. This assessment works only if it is a mirror, not a brochure.

Recognition levels

Foundational

Average score ≥ 1.0 and every critical Tier-1 standard scores at least 1. The basics exist, even if informal.

Operational

Average ≥ 2.0; critical Tier 1–2 standards score at least 2; evidence recorded for ≥ 80% of applicable standards.

Exemplary

Average ≥ 2.7; all critical standards at 3; ≥ 95% evidence; three years of retained records. Practices are sustained and review-ready — the level a peer-review or recognition program could later build on.

Important: this is a working draft for pilot testing — not a legal, code-compliance, or insurance certification. Local fire, building, occupational-safety, employment, youth-protection, environmental, and insurance requirements vary; verify them with qualified local professionals.

Standards assessment

Each standard shows an example of what a 0, 1, 2, and 3 look like in practice — click the description that best matches your space today. Then attach evidence: links to documents, small file uploads, or written notes (a document title with date, a log range, a verifiable observation — not a general assertion). Evidence stays private unless you mark an item “Share as resource” to offer it to other spaces as a template.

Scoring rubric

0 — Absent. No reliable practice or evidence.

1 — Foundational. Basic practice exists; may be informal or inconsistent; minimum evidence available.

2 — Operational. Written, assigned, consistently implemented, supported by current evidence.

3 — Sustained / review-ready. Effectiveness is reviewed; improvements documented; historical evidence supports continuity.
